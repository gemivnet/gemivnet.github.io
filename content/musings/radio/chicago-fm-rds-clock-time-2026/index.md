Some radios can set their clock from RDS. That
only works if the station sends clock time and sends it right. Nobody publishes
which Chicago stations do, so I listened to all of them.

## What RDS clock time is

Clock time (CT) is RDS group 4A. A station sends one per minute, at the start of
the minute. It carries the UTC date and time to the minute, plus a local offset
in half hours. The radio adds the offset to get local time, so a station that
sends UTC with a zero offset sets your radio five or six hours off. Stations that
don't feed the RDS encoder a time source drift.

## Results

Four stations send correct clock time: WTMX 101.9, WKQX 101.1, WCFS 105.9 and
WAWE 94.3. WAWE lands about 6 seconds after the minute against 1 to 2.5 for the
other three, so its clock runs a few seconds late, likely because the encoder
sits behind a satellite feed. Fine for a bedside radio.

Three send it wrong. WBEZ 91.5's clock is 7 hours 27 minutes behind UTC and
sent with a zero offset, so a radio shows about 2 hours 27 minutes slow. WPNA
103.1 is 21.5 minutes slow. WMBI 90.1 sends the right UTC with a zero offset, so
a radio that applies the offset is five hours fast.

One is ambiguous. WSCR 104.3 sends local time labelled as UTC: right on radios
that apply the offset as sent, five hours off on radios that convert from UTC
themselves.

Every other station with RDS sends no clock time.

To set a clock, use 101.9, 101.1 or 105.9. All three are strong, decode cleanly,
and land within about two seconds of the minute. These results are from Central
Daylight Time; whether each station follows the switch to standard time on
November 1 is untested.

I've reached out to the stations sending wrong clock time and will update this
page if I hear back.

## Reading the tables

- **Station**: the licensed station on that frequency according to radio-locator,
  cross-checked against Playlist Research and RadioLineup.
- **CT**: *correct* is UTC within a minute and the right offset for Chicago.
  *Right UTC, wrong offset* means a radio that applies the offset is hours off.
  *Local time sent as UTC* is right on radios that apply the offset and wrong on
  ones that convert from UTC themselves. *None* means no clock time in 130
  seconds. A "none" on a weak signal is a weaker claim, since a clock time group
  only counts if it arrives without errors.
- **Radio shows**: what a radio that applies the offset as sent
  ends up displaying.
- **RDS signal**: how many of the expected 11.4 groups per second arrived, and the
  block error rate before correction.
- **PS**: the 8-character station name. "Scrolling" means the station pushes
  song titles through it.
- **PI call**: RBDS works a call sign out of the PI code. iHeart and others use
  PI codes that don't map to their call, so "none" or "mismatch" is a fact about
  the station, not a wrong ID. Two cases look odd and aren't. WKQX sends 0xA7C7,
  NRSC-4's zero-nibble form of 0x70C7, which is WKQX. WLS-FM sends 0x997D, the
  three-letter code for WLS; redsea 1.3.1 misses most three-letter codes (its
  table is searched as if sorted and isn't), so the survey looks those up itself.
- **Garbled 4A**: on a noisy stretch, error correction now and then "repairs" a
  group into a clock time dated 1901 or 2206. Those are thrown out and counted,
  never used for a verdict.

## Method

- Receivers: a variety of RTL-SDR dongles and antennas on the Southwest Side,
  near Midway, listed under the tables. The ADS-B gear isn't built for FM; these
  stations are strong enough that it doesn't matter much.
- Decoder: [redsea](https://github.com/windytan/redsea) 1.3.1 in RBDS mode, fed
  171 kHz MPX from `rtl_fm`. Receive clock synced by NTP.
- 12 seconds on every channel from 87.9 to 107.9, then 130 seconds (two minute
  boundaries) on every channel with RDS. Listed stations that showed no RDS got
  another 40 seconds.
- One pass in October, on Central Daylight Time (-05:00). It doesn't show which
  stations follow the switch to standard time on November 1.
- To run it in your own market: [survey.sh](media/survey.sh) and
  [scan.sh](media/scan.sh). Every decoded group is in the raw files above,
  timestamped on receipt.
